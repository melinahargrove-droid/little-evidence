Social-Emotional · Objective 1a

Manages Feelings

With growing independence, the child manages feelings and uses strategies to feel safe, calm, and ready to take part.

Ready to begin?

Think about real classroom moments. No child task is needed for this objective.

View objective guideProgression · examples · evidence tips

↗ Developmental progression

♡ Examples around Level 6

✓Waits briefly for a turn or a wanted item with adult reassurance.
✓Adjusts when a plan changes or a preferred activity ends.
✓Tries another plan after a small disappointment.
✓Accepts a familiar calming reminder and returns to play.

Look for the child’s own response, what support helped, and whether the skill appears across routines and time. A SmartBoard task is not appropriate for this objective.

⌕ How to collect evidence

Observe authentic moments such as waiting, transitions, a change in plan, or a minor frustration. Notice what happened just before, what the child did, and what support was needed. Add notes or media only when useful.

✧ Quick reflection

What feelings or situations are easiest for the child to manage? What support helps them recover or rejoin play?

Materials to gatherFor everyday observation
  • No special materials are required; observe real moments in classroom routines and play.
  • Optional: familiar emotion pictures, storybooks, or calming tools already used in class.

Teacher observation guide · Objective 1a

Notice feelings in real moments

This is a teacher-facing guide. Do not create distress or ask the child to perform a feelings task. Notice naturally occurring moments and respond with the support the child needs.

Keep it specific: Capture the situation, the child’s action or words, the support offered, and what happened next. One moment is a starting point; use evidence across days and settings for a checkpoint.
Look for · 1

When something is disappointing

For example, a toy is unavailable or a turn is delayed. Does the child seek comfort, wait, adjust, or try another plan?

Look for · 2

During a transition

Notice what helps the child shift away from a preferred activity and how they communicate their feelings.

Look for · 3

After a strong feeling

Watch whether the child accepts support, uses a calming strategy, and returns to play or a routine.

Social-Emotional · Objective 1b

Follows Limits and Expectations

Notices, follows, and begins to understand familiar expectations in the classroom and beyond.

Ready to begin?

Think about everyday rules, redirection, and transitions. No SmartBoard task is needed.

View objective guideProgression · examples · evidence tips

Developmental progression

NY
Not YetNeeds substantial adult support to notice or respond to limits.
1–2
Notices and responds to adult cuesWatches adult actions and responds to changes in tone or expression.
3–4
Responds to redirectionBegins to change behavior after an adult reminder and accepts redirection.
5–6
Follows familiar expectationsManages classroom rules, routines, and transitions with reminders.
7–8
Applies expectations in similar new situationsUses familiar rules in another classroom or group situation.
9–10
Understands why rules matterExplains reasons for rules, sometimes with a prompt or example.
11–12
Understands broad “big rule” ideasReasons about safety, kindness, respect, and caring for people and materials.

Odd-numbered points reflect emerging or in-between evidence. Confirm with the official GOLD continuum and observations over time.

Examples to notice

✓Accepts an adult's redirection and changes what they are doing.
✓Follows familiar classroom routines with only occasional reminders.
✓Uses a familiar expectation in a new but similar setting.
✓Explains that a rule helps keep people safe or materials cared for.

How to collect evidence

Observe regular classroom routines, transitions, adult redirection, and familiar expectations used in a new situation. Notice what the child does before and after a reminder, and whether understanding carries across adults and settings.

Optional child prompt

For higher-level understanding, ask in conversation: “Why can we run outside but not in the hallway?” or “What does it mean to take care of our classroom?” A spoken response supports your observation; it is never required.

Materials to gatherFor everyday observation
  • No special materials are required; observe classroom rules, transitions, and routines.
  • Optional: the classroom visual schedule or familiar expectation pictures.
